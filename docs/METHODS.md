# How it works (full notes)

These notes give each rule behind the summary above in detail. Numbers like (Q9) refer to the list of open questions; click one to open that row.

## Starting points

- **Year.** Income earned in calendar 2026. Income tax uses the 令和8年分 rules; resident tax is the 令和9年度 tax that falls on 2026 income.
- **Place.** Shinjuku ward, Tokyo: 協会けんぽ (Japan Health Insurance Association) Tokyo rates for employees, Shinjuku rules for National Health Insurance and resident tax. Outside Shinjuku, National Health Insurance and parts of resident tax (set by each municipality) differ; outside Tokyo, so does the health insurance rate for employees (set by prefecture).
- **Households.** Eight example households (H1–H8), the same for every plan. Their incomes and families are shown on the Compare page.
- **Take-home pay** = salary, business and pension income + child allowance + single-parent allowance − income tax − resident tax − social insurance (including the long-term care premium for people aged 65 or over).
- **People don't change how they work** when the rules change (a static estimate).

## 2026 rules (before any plan)

### Income tax (令和8年分)
- Salary income is salary minus the employment-income deduction. In 2026 that deduction is at least ¥740,000, so below ¥2,191,000 salary income is salary − ¥740,000 (NTA, 令和8年分 年末調整のしかた).
- Basic deduction: ¥1,040,000 at total income up to ¥4.89m, ¥670,000 up to ¥6.55m, ¥620,000 up to ¥23.5m.
- Then the spouse deductions, dependant deductions (¥380,000 at 16+, ¥630,000 at 19–22, ¥480,000 at 70+, or ¥580,000 for a parent or grandparent aged 70+ living with the taxpayer), the special deduction for relatives aged 19–22, the single-parent and working-student deductions, and social insurance.
- Income adjustment deduction (所得金額調整控除): for people with salary over ¥8.5m and a dependant under 23, and for people with both salary income and pension income (up to ¥100,000 of each, minus ¥100,000). It comes off salary income (NTA Tax Answer No.1411).
- The 2.1% reconstruction surtax is added and the total rounded down to ¥100.

### Resident tax (令和9年度)
- Deduction amounts and income limits come from the 地方税法 as amended by 令和8年法律第2号: basic deduction ¥430,000; income limit for a spouse or dependant ¥620,000; a dependant aged 70+ ¥380,000 (¥450,000 for a parent or grandparent living with the taxpayer).
- Ward tax (6%) and Tokyo tax (4%) are worked out separately and each rounded down to ¥100 (地方税法20条の4の2第3項). The adjustment credit (調整控除, which offsets the gap between income-tax and resident-tax deductions) is also taken off each separately.
- The flat per-person part is ¥5,000 (ward ¥3,000, Tokyo ¥1,000, forest environment tax ¥1,000). A single person with income up to ¥450,000, among others, pays no resident tax; a single parent pays none up to income ¥1,350,000.

### Company insurance (health, pension, employment insurance, child and childcare support levy)
- 協会けんぽ Tokyo's fiscal 2026 premium table. Premiums are set by pay band (標準報酬月額), so they step up when pay crosses into the next band.
- Part-timers: non-students working 20+ hours a week at a firm with 51+ employees are in company insurance when paid ¥88,000 a month or more, for all 12 months. The ¥88,000 condition ended on 1 October 2026 (令和8年政令第275号), but at 20 hours a week even the lowest regional minimum wage (¥1,023 in October–December) pays more than ¥88,000 a month, so only workers with a minimum-wage exception are paid less. They join only if they ask (厚生年金保険法附則4条の6, 健康保険法附則8条の3の2). (Approximation) Nobody asks.
- Non-students working 20+ hours a week pay employment insurance whether or not they are in company health insurance and pension (雇用保険法6条 has no pay condition).

### National Health Insurance and National Pension
- NHI follows Shinjuku's fiscal 2026 method and is paid by the head for the household. Low-income households get 70%, 50% or 20% off. An "earner" (給与所得者等) in that test is someone with salary over ¥550,000, or, without salary income, a public pension over ¥600,000 (¥1,250,000 at 65+) (国民健康保険法施行令29条の7第6項第1号, 附則5条).
- National Pension is ¥17,920 a month. A spouse who is a dependant of an employee doesn't pay it.
- The student deferral (学生納付特例, which lets a student pay later) applies only while the student's own income, after social insurance, is at most ¥1,280,000 (国民年金法施行令6条の9). (Approximation) It is judged on the same year's income (in law, the previous year's).
- People on low incomes can apply to be let off National Pension, but that lowers their future pension, so we assume nobody applies (Q20).

### Long-term care premium for people aged 65 or over
- Shinjuku's fiscal 2026 premiums (18 bands, standard ¥79,200 a year). People who pay no resident tax are in five bands (¥19,800 to ¥79,200, by pension plus other income and whether anyone else in the household pays resident tax); people who pay it are in thirteen bands by total income (¥87,120 to ¥459,360). The premium is deducted from that person's income for tax.
- (Approximation) The band uses the same year's income and resident-tax status (in law, the previous year's).

### Benefits
- Child allowance: ¥10,000 a month per child aged 18 or under (¥15,000 under 3, ¥30,000 from the third child), with no income limit.
- Single-parent allowance (児童扶養手当): ¥48,050 a month for the first child and ¥11,350 for each further child (from April 2026), for a single parent with a child up to the first 31 March after turning 18 (or under 20 with a disability). When the income used for the allowance (salary income plus public pension income, minus ¥100,000, then minus ¥80,000: 施行令4条) is at or above a limit (¥690,000 plus ¥380,000 per dependant, ¥530,000 for one aged 16–22, ¥480,000 for one aged 70+) it falls by 2.64% of the excess for the first child and 0.41% for each further child, rounded to ¥10, and it stops at ¥2,080,000 plus the same additions (児童扶養手当法施行令2条の2, 2条の4, 4条). A public pension of the parent is taken off month by month (施行令6条の5). It is not taxed. (Approximation) The April 2026 amounts for all 12 months, judged on the same year's income; the mother receives it and gets no child support payments.

## How each plan is calculated

Each plan uses only the numbers from its own documents, on top of the 2026 rules. The same household is worked out twice, under 2026 rules and with the plan, and the difference in take-home pay is split into five parts: income tax, resident tax, social insurance, payments or refunds received, and consumption tax.

- **Daiwa Institute of Research.** The income-tax basic deduction is abolished and each person gets a tax credit instead. Daiwa's formula sets it: (the ¥1,780,000 tax threshold − the minimum employment-income deduction − the basic deduction under the plan, 0) × 5%. On 2026 rules that is (¥1,780,000 − ¥740,000 − 0) × 5% = ¥52,000 (Daiwa's ¥56,500 is the same formula on 2025 rules). Credit left after income tax is paid in cash, up to the social insurance the person pays on their work: health insurance (with long-term care) and pension for people in company insurance; National Health Insurance and National Pension for other workers; and, for workers aged 65 or over, the long-term care premium. People who live mainly on a pension, income from assets, or by drawing down their assets have no social insurance on income from work, so they get no cash.
  - (Approximation) The credit comes off before the 2.1% surtax (Q11). The child and childcare support levy and employment insurance don't count toward the cash limit (Q18). NHI is paid by the head for the household, so it counts only toward the head's limit, and only if the head works. When the higher earner pays and deducts the spouse's National Pension, it still counts toward the spouse's own limit (Daiwa doesn't cover this case).
- **Government payment (the form from FY2029)** and **the payment in the FY2027–28 plan.** The shape of the payment (flat at low income, rising with income, flat, an extra per child, falling at high income, rounded up to ¥1,000) is in the program and tested with made-up numbers. Under the bill, the real amounts and rates will be set later by Cabinet order, so no number is shown (Q9). The temporary top-up for people facing an income wall (the extra premiums of joining company insurance or of moving into National Health Insurance and National Pension) and others (就業促進特例加算) is not included either: the outline says its amount will be set considering the overall balance, among other things, and under the bill the payment amounts are to be set by Cabinet order. Of the bill's conditions, the tests on the tax and insurance paid and on the spouse's income aren't in the program yet either, because their limits haven't been set.
- **The food tax cut in the FY2027–28 plan.** Tax saved = food spending × (8% − 1%) ÷ 1.08 × the share passed on to prices. 8% is today's reduced rate (NTA Tax Answer No.6303); 1% is the rate in the outline. (Approximation) The whole cut is passed on to prices. Food spending is loaded for the all-household estimate but not yet for the example households H1–H8 (Q8). The tax cut and the payment are one plan, so no number is shown for the tax side alone until the payment amounts are decided.
- **Rengo.** From Rengo's Tax Reform Plan (4th edition, revised, 28 May 2025) and its 2026 key policies. Of its two parts, the consumption-tax refund's amounts (per-person amount, or how it falls as income rises) haven't been set out yet, so it can't be calculated (Q3). The plan also abolishes the reduced 8% rate on food, so it needs food spending for the example households (Q8). The other part (the work-support credit) is not shown on its own, because that would show only half of the plan.

## Income wall charts

One person's income goes from ¥0 to ¥10,000,000 in ¥10,000 steps, and take-home pay is worked out at each step. In two-income households the moving income is fixed: H4 the spouse's salary; H3 and H8 the head's salary; H6 the head's pension. Nothing else about the household changes.

- The wall list shows the three largest places where earning ¥10,000 more cuts take-home pay by ¥10,000 or more, each with its cause: the change in each tax, premium and benefit, and any rule that starts or stops there.
- The second chart shows how much more take-home pay an extra ¥100,000 of income brings, at each ¥100,000. A narrow wall may not go below ¥0 there, so the listed walls are also marked ▼ on the take-home chart.
- Both leave out the step up from an income of ¥0 (a full-time employee paid nothing is not a real case).
- "Take-home gets back to where it was" is the first ¥10,000 step where it does, so the real crossing can be up to ¥10,000 lower.

## All-household estimate (every number is an approximation)

- **Data.** Households by type and ¥500,000 income band, with each band's average earnings, business income, pensions, ages and number of earners, from the 2024 National Survey of Family Income, Consumption and Wealth (令和6年全国家計構造調査, 所得 第3-32-1表); food spending from the same survey (家計収支 第1-21表). Other official tables are used only as checks (Checks page).
- **Household types.** Single; couple only; couple with one, two, or three or more children; lone parent with children; couple living with parents; other households of two or more.
- **Made-up households.** For each type and income band, households matching the band's averages:
  - split into households living on pensions and households living on work, in the same ratio as pensions to total income;
  - the survey's earners, after the heads of households living on work, are spread evenly over the spouses in those households and over other adults, so some households have a working spouse or adult child and some don't (spouses in households living on pensions don't work); earnings are set so the survey's averages stay the same;
  - children under 18 are aged 10, adult children 22, people living on a pension 70; family members aged 70+ other than the head and spouse are treated as a parent or grandparent of the head or spouse living with them.
- **Results.** Every made-up household is worked out with the same rules as the example households. Households are sorted by annual income and cut into ten groups of equal size; for each group, the average change in take-home pay and the shares of households that gain, lose or see no change.
- **Cost.** The national total of the change in take-home pay: the government's net cost before any financing, assuming nobody changes how they work. Shown next to the proposer's own estimate where one is published.
- **Not included:** the gap between 2024 (survey) and 2026 (rules), income from rent, interest and dividends, and differences between households in the same type and income band.

## Build your own

The "Build your own" page lets a visitor set the rules of a simple refundable tax credit with five sliders: the rate it grows at, the maximum per person, the amount per child, the income where it starts to fall, and the rate it falls at.

- It is worked out per person. A person with earnings (salary + business income) gets earnings × the growth rate, up to the maximum. In a couple, the partner with the higher earnings (the head if equal) also gets the amount per child for each child aged 18 or under. Above the income where it starts to fall (income = earnings + pensions, before tax), it falls by the rate × the excess. It never goes below ¥0 and is rounded down to the yen.
- The credit is added to take-home pay under 2026 rules. It is not taxed and changes no other tax, insurance or benefit.
- The all-household numbers use the same made-up households as the "All households" page, so they are all approximations.
- The starting values are placeholders for the sliders and come from no plan.

## List of approximations

- Health and long-term care rates that start in March 2026, employment insurance from April, and the child and childcare support levy that is new from April are applied to all 12 months (the pension rate, 18.3%, does not change).
- NHI uses Shinjuku's fiscal 2026 rates on 2026 income (fiscal 2027 rates are not published).
- The employee's shares of health insurance and of the child and childcare support levy are each rounded (a government Q&A said to round them together, but it has been withdrawn; Q38).
- Salary is paid in 12 equal months with no bonus; each child's age stays the same for the whole year.
- In a couple, the higher earner claims the spouse and the children (Q23).
- The student deferral, the 65+ care premium, the single-parent allowance, part-time coverage, the Daiwa plan, the food tax cut and the all-household estimate have their own approximations, listed with each rule above.
- Not included: changes in how people work; the parents of the student household H2 (Q16).

## Neutrality

Every plan uses the same households, data and method. Results are stated as findings; the site doesn't recommend any plan, party or politician. When a plan can't be calculated, the reason is given as it is (amounts to be set later by Cabinet order, or not set out yet, or data this site doesn't have yet).

## The website and how to reproduce it

- Every number can be rebuilt by getting the repository and running `make all`. The 25 pages from other publishers that can't be stored in the repository (listed in `sources/EXTERNAL.tsv`) are downloaded during the build and checked against a saved fingerprint. If a publisher has replaced one of them, the copy saved at the Internet Archive is used, checked the same way; 11 of the 25 pages have such a copy. If one of the others is replaced, the build stops and names the page. The exception is three 協会けんぽ pages: their file differs on every download because of a code embedded in the page (the text shown stays the same), so they are used as downloaded, without a fingerprint check. Rates and amounts are in `parameters/` (each with its source), the example households in `households/`, and the hand calculations in `tests/hand_calcs/`.
- The result pages read their numbers from the calculation output files (`outputs/`) when they open. On the Checks page, the numbers in the tables comparing with another calculator, the income-wall table and the calibration tables are checked against those outputs by tests; numbers in the text around them are kept up to date by hand.
- On a first visit the site is in Japanese if the browser's language is Japanese, and in English otherwise; a button switches language.
- Chart colours follow a colour-blind-safe palette (Okabe-Ito) and carry no meaning. Charts have text labels, and the Compare page repeats every charted number in a table.
- No tracking or analytics.
